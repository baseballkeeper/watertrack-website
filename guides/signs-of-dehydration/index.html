Signs of Dehydration in Adults

Common symptoms, warning signs that need medical care, and steps to take when you may be short on fluids.

Published November 1, 2026 · By WaterTrack Editorial, MPM Ventures LLC

Common signs of dehydration in adults

Dehydration happens when fluid losses exceed fluid intake. Symptoms can include:

  • Feeling unusually thirsty
  • Dark yellow or strong-smelling urine
  • Urinating less frequently than normal
  • Dry mouth, lips, or tongue
  • Headache, tiredness, or lightheadedness
  • Dizziness, particularly when standing

These signs are useful clues, but none proves dehydration by itself. Urine color can also change because of medication, supplements, and food. Symptoms may overlap with other conditions.

When dehydration needs medical attention

Seek prompt medical advice if dizziness on standing does not resolve, urine output drops substantially, you cannot keep fluids down, or symptoms persist despite drinking. Rapid breathing or a fast heart rate can also warrant urgent assessment.

Emergency warning: Confusion, difficulty breathing, being unusually hard to wake, or signs of shock require immediate emergency help. In the United States, call 911.

Babies, young children, and older adults are more vulnerable. This article focuses on adults and should not be used to assess dehydration in an infant.

What can increase the risk?

Dehydration can develop during hot weather, prolonged exercise, fever, vomiting, or diarrhea. Some medications, including diuretics, increase fluid loss. People who depend on others for access to drinks can also be at higher risk.

If you have heart, kidney, or other conditions requiring fluid management, ask your healthcare team for individualized instructions.

What to do if you think you're mildly dehydrated

If you're alert, able to swallow, and symptoms are mild, drink fluids in manageable amounts. If nausea makes drinking difficult, try small sips. Vomiting or diarrhea can cause loss of salts as well as water; a pharmacist or healthcare professional can advise on oral rehydration solutions.

Do not force large volumes of plain water quickly. If symptoms are severe or worsening, seek care rather than relying on a hydration app.

Can tracking help?

A drink log may help you notice whether you routinely go long stretches without beverages. It cannot measure your body's hydration status or diagnose dehydration. For practical recordkeeping, see how to track water intake. For general reference values, see how much water adults need.
