How Much Water Should You Drink Per Day?

Understand the common daily water intake numbers, what they include, and how to use them without treating them as a strict quota.

Published November 1, 2026 · By WaterTrack Editorial, MPM Ventures LLC
Quick answer: The U.S. National Academies sets adequate intake levels for total water at about 3.7 liters (125 fl oz) daily for adult men and 2.7 liters (91 fl oz) for adult women. These amounts include water from food and all beverages. They are population reference values, not personal drinking quotas.

How much water per day is recommended?

There is no single number that suits everyone. The National Academies' adequate intake figures describe amounts commonly consumed by healthy adults who appear adequately hydrated. They do not mean that every person must reach that exact amount each day.

Adult groupTotal water per dayApproximate US fluid ounces
Men, 19+3.7 liters125 fl oz
Women, 19+2.7 liters91 fl oz

These are general reference values for healthy adults. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, illness, medications, and some medical conditions can change what is appropriate. If you've been given a fluid restriction, follow that medical advice rather than a general chart.

Total water is different from the water you drink

The 125-ounce and 91-ounce figures include water from your meals, coffee, tea, milk, and other drinks. The National Academies' survey data found that roughly one-fifth of total water came from food, although the proportion varies by diet.

That distinction matters. Drinking 125 ounces of plain water on top of all your other beverages and food would misinterpret the reference value.

For adults ages 19 to 30, the survey data underlying the report included about 3.0 liters of beverages for men and 2.2 liters for women, with the remainder from food. These are observed averages, not individual prescriptions.

When you may need more or less

Fluid needs change from day to day. Hot weather, physical activity, fever, vomiting, and diarrhea can increase fluid losses. Pregnancy and breastfeeding also affect needs. A clinician may recommend limiting fluids for certain medical conditions.

Thirst and regular access to drinks with meals are useful guides for many healthy adults. Urine color can provide another clue, but supplements, medications, and some foods can affect it.

A practical way to drink consistently

  1. Have a drink with breakfast and each main meal.
  2. Keep a reusable bottle where you work or study.
  3. Refill after activity or time outdoors, adjusting to your thirst and conditions.
  4. Notice patterns over several days rather than chasing one exact number.

Common questions

Is eight glasses of water a day enough?

Eight 8-ounce glasses equal 64 fluid ounces. That may suit some people, but it is not a universal requirement. Other beverages and food also contribute water.

Does coffee count?

Yes. Coffee and tea contribute to total water intake. Water remains a useful default choice, especially if you're trying to limit added sugar.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes. Consuming excessive water, especially rapidly, can be dangerous. Avoid forcing large amounts to meet an arbitrary goal. Seek medical guidance for endurance exercise, significant illness, or fluid restrictions.
